Core build order
  1. Identify your primary stack game first. Highest over/under on the slate where both SplashPlay and Stokastic project a team higher than the field's likely ownership suggests.
  2. Build a 4-5 man stack from that team, ideally consecutive batting order spots (1-5) so they come to the plate together in big innings.
  3. Add a bring-back — 1-2 hitters from the opposing team in that same game. Correlation hedge if the game turns into a slugfest on both sides.
  4. Pick your SP from a completely different game than your stack. Your SP should face a weak/low-implied-total offense. Never let your SP face your own stack's hitters.
  5. Fill remaining spots with a mini-stack from a third game (DK) or best-available upside play (FD, tighter roster).
Where SplashPlay vs Stokastic disagreement helps

In cash, disagreement between sources is a red flag — stick to consensus. In GPP it's the opposite: when SplashPlay projects a team or player significantly higher than Stokastic (or vice versa), that's a signal the field may be under-rostering them. Dig into why — park factors, recent role change, platoon advantage, weather. If there's a real catalyst, that's free leverage. If there's no reason for the gap, treat it with more caution.

Slate size adjustments

Small slate (1-4 games)

  • Field convergence is extreme — everyone sees the same 2-3 stackable teams.
  • Hard to avoid SP vs. own-stack conflicts; fewer games means more overlap.
  • Your best (often only) lever is a contrarian SP both sources like but isn't the chalk arm.
  • Reduce GPP budget and entry count — edge compression makes outcomes closer to random.
  • If down to ~1 reliable game (weather risk on others), consider skipping GPP entirely and playing cash only.

Large slate (10+ games)

  • More stacking options = more ways to differentiate from the field.
  • Easier to find a SP/stack combo with zero correlation conflict.
  • Ownership projections matter more — the field spreads out, so true contrarian plays exist.
  • Good slate to run your full 3-lineup framework (primary / secondary / contrarian).
Field size adjustments (your typical fields: 10 / 100 / 1,000 / 2,000+)
Field sizeWhat it meansAdjustment
~10 entries Tiny field. A handful of sharp players can dominate; near-chalk builds often cash since the "field" barely diverges. Light differentiation needed. A strong consensus-leaning build with 1 leverage piece is usually enough. Don't over-engineer.
~100 entries Small-mid field. Some convergence on obvious plays, but real ownership spread exists. One clear point of difference (SP or stack) is usually sufficient. Full 3-lineup framework starts to make sense if budget allows.
~1,000 entries True GPP dynamics. Top-heavy payouts, meaningful ownership data, real leverage spots exist. Run the full 3-lineup framework: primary stack, secondary stack (different game), contrarian build. Use ownership + disagreement signals fully.
2,000+ entries Large field. To win outright you need a build very few others have. Chalk gets you min-cash at best. Lean harder into contrarian SP + secondary stack from an overlooked game. Accept lower median outcome in exchange for top-heavy ceiling. Consider multiple differentiated entries if budget allows (3-Max, 5-Max).

General rule: differentiation should scale with field size. A build that's perfectly fine for a 10-person field can be hopelessly chalky for a 2,000-entry GPP, and an overly-contrarian build for a 10-person field just throws away expected value for no reason.

$20/day budget framework
  • 3-4 lineups max. Enough to cover different game environments without losing track of late swaps.
  • Different pitcher in every lineup. The single most important differentiation rule — if all lineups share an SP and he gets shelled, everything dies together.
  • Different bring-back games per lineup so your ceiling scenarios aren't all riding on the same game script.
  • Enter each lineup once in a $1 single-entry contest where possible (forces commitment, removes the late-swap burden of multi-entry), with remainder in one small multi-entry (3-Max / 5-Max).
Weather / rainout handling
  • Confirmed PPD before lock → exclude all players from that game entirely. DK/FD void and refund those lineups, but only if postponed before lock.
  • Rain risk, not confirmed → don't exclude outright. Reduce exposure — e.g. only 1 of your 3-4 lineups leans on that game.
  • Down to effectively 1 game → sit out GPP. Can't differentiate from one game's player pool; it becomes a coin flip, not an edge. Cash only, or skip the slate.
  • Check weather 60-90 minutes before lock — earlier forecasts change too much to act on.
